Particle System

  • Particle Engine
    Demonstrates the particle engine, running on the main thread, bound to the engine's update/render loop. Keeping particle limits low and effect sizes limited reduces overhead to an acceptable performance level.
  • WebWorkers
    The particle engine can utilize WebWorkers if the browser supports them. The particle engine is distributed to worker threads, sharing the burden, with an Orchestrator to manage the load and the particle coalesce and render operation.
    Running with WebWorkers can free up load in the engine's update/render loop for more active objects in the scene.
  • Particle types & effect
    The particle engine is flexible with base types for particles and effects, but extensible with a simple inehritance scheme. Particle types reduce the amount of state data needed, per particle, keeping the update/render operation fast.
    Particle effects generate particles and influence their spawing behavior to give the ultimate flexibility in the design and generation of repeatable particle effects.
    Included types:
    • BasicParticle - position, velocity, drag, dragRate, and gravity
    • AffectedParticle - Like basic, but affected by repulsors and colliders (increased CPU load)
    Included effects:
    • Explosion - Configurable color palette, particle sizes, origin smoke properties, renders with points, 360° emission
    • Puff - Configurable color palette, particle sizes, renders with diffuse points, 360° emission
    • Fountain - Configurable color palette, particle sizes, renders with points or sprites, spread-angle emission
    • Fireworks - Pre-defined color palettes, uses points to render, spawns sub-particles, built-in types: Peony, palm, brocade, comet, etc.
    • Spark - Configurable color palette, streak length, renders with lines and points, spread-angle emission
    • Smoke - Configurable color palette, renders with diffuse points, rises slowly with a narrow spread-angle emission
  • Particle repulsors
    Particle repulsors are diffuse areas of influence over live particles. Each repulsor adds impulse to the particles that are within its range of influence. Repulsors are circular in shape with an effective strength that is greater near the origin of the repulsor. Repulsors add impulse to the particles via simple alteration of values, or a complex function that can generate turbulence or other effects. Additional processing always imposes extra CPU overhead.
  • Particle colliders
    Particle colliders are areas where particles cannot enter. Defined as rectangles or circles, when particles encounter a collider their movement stops or reflects, based on the collider's properies. Colliders allow for particles to visually interact with the game environment. Additional processing imposes extra CPU overhead.
  • Particle occlusion

    The particle engine runs separate from the game engine. Its only purpose is to move and render particles, so it doesn't care about the game's objects or surfaces. You can use repulsors and colliders, sparingly, to create the effects of environment interaction, but particles just render across the entire viewport unless blocked.

    That's where occlusion comes in... When objects in the game update, they can utilize the ParticleOccluder component to automatically generate an occlusion shape. With RenderContexts supporting multi-planar rendering (for parallax support or depth-of-field) objects with a ParticleOccluder component will generate an occluder based on their rendering-plane: none for background, hinted for middle, and assumed on for front.

    Occluders are collected into an occlusion mask that is applied to the particles as they are rendered into the scene. Occluders can create the effect of depth for the particles generated.
